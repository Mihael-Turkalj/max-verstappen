# Verstappen four-title run (2021–2024): verified fact file

For an unofficial fan showcase site. Research date: **29 Sep 2026**. All web sources were accessed that day.

**Conventions**
- "Wins", "poles", "podiums" and "fastest laps" count **Grands Prix only**, not sprints. Season **points** include sprint points and fastest-lap bonus points.
- "Pole" means the **credited pole**, i.e. the driver who started from P1 or who was credited under that year's rules. Where Verstappen was fastest in qualifying but did not start first, this is noted. See §9.
- Figures with no StatsF1 or formula1.com backing are tagged **(Wikipedia only)**, **(single source)** or **UNVERIFIED**.

## 2. Signature races

### 2.1 2021: Abu Dhabi finale (12 Dec 2021, round 22 of 22)

- **Before the race:** Verstappen and Hamilton were level on 369.5 points. This is derived from the final totals [2] minus the Abu Dhabi points [17], and matches Wikipedia's "tied on points" [48].
- **Start:** Verstappen started on pole [17]. Hamilton took the lead at the start [48].
- **Lap 53 of 58:** Nicholas Latifi crashed at Turn 14 and the Safety Car was deployed. Verstappen pitted for new soft tyres. Hamilton stayed out on hard tyres [37].
- **Lap 56:** Race Control published "LAPPED CARS WILL NOT BE ALLOWED TO OVERTAKE" [37].
- **Lap 57:** Only the **five** lapped cars running between Hamilton and Verstappen (NOR, ALO, OCO, LEC, VET) were told to overtake the Safety Car. Three other lapped cars behind Verstappen were not. "SAFETY CAR IN THIS LAP" followed, and racing resumed for the final lap [37].
- **Lap 58:** Verstappen passed Hamilton at Turn 5 and won by 2.256 s [17][19][37]. It was his first title, and Red Bull's first drivers' title since 2013 [58].
- **Protests:** Mercedes lodged two protests.
  - The first alleged overtaking under the Safety Car.
  - The second alleged that the restart procedure breached Article 48.12.
  - The stewards dismissed both. On the second, they found that Article 48.12 "may not have been applied fully" but that Article 48.13 overrode it, and that amending the classification would be "effectively shortening the race retrospectively" [37].
- **Appeal:** Mercedes gave notice of appeal, then publicly withdrew it on 16 Dec 2021 [37].
- **Race director:** on 17 Feb 2022 the FIA replaced Michael Masi as race director. Niels Wittich and Eduardo Freitas were to alternate in the role [20].
- **FIA report (19 Mar 2022):**
  - Articles 48.12 and 48.13 were open to "different interpretations" [37].
  - The FIA said "human error" meant not all lapped cars were allowed to unlap, and that the race director acted "in good faith" [40].
  - The results are "valid, final and cannot now be changed" [37][40].
- **Suggested neutral framing:** the finish was decided by a late Safety Car restart. The FIA later acknowledged that the procedure was not applied as written, but the result stands.

### 2.2 2022: Suzuka clinch (9 Oct 2022, round 18 of 22)

- **Race:** it started in rain and was red-flagged after 2 laps. After the restart it ended at the time limit after **28 of 53** laps [17][21][49]. Verstappen won by 27.066 s [17].
- **Leclerc penalty:** Leclerc got a 5-second penalty for cutting the final chicane on the last lap. That dropped him from 2nd to 3rd, behind Pérez, and made Verstappen champion [21].
- **Full points:** full points were awarded. The 2022 reduced-points rule applied only if a suspended race "could not be resumed", and this one had resumed. Much of the paddock had expected reduced points [42].
- **Races left: 4** (United States, Mexico City, São Paulo, Abu Dhabi) [4][21].
- **Deficit overturned:** earlier in 2022 he had trailed Leclerc by 46 points after the Australian GP. This was a record comeback [41].

### 2.3 2023: the 10-race streak, 19/22, 21/22 and the Qatar sprint

- **Streak (10):** Miami (7 May) → Monaco → Spain → Canada → Austria → Great Britain → Hungary → Belgium → Netherlands → **Italy (3 Sep)** [7][25].
  - It beat Sebastian Vettel's 9 from 2013 [25][44].
  - It ended when Sainz won at Singapore (17 Sep) [7].
- **Win rate:** 19 of 22 = **86.36%** [57]. formula1.com gives "86.3%" [26] and Autosport "86%" [44]; both are rounded.
- **Red Bull:** won **21 of 22** GPs (Verstappen 19, Pérez 2). Singapore was the only defeat [7].
  - That is 95.45% [50][60] ("95.4%" in [26]). It beat McLaren's 1988 MP4/4 record of 15 of 16 [26][60].
  - Red Bull won 15 consecutive races, from Abu Dhabi 2022 to Italy 2023 [25].
- **Qatar sprint clinch (Sat 7 Oct 2023):**
  - He needed 6th in the 19-lap sprint and finished **2nd** behind Piastri [18][23].
  - This put the title beyond Pérez, who retired from the sprint [23].
  - It came with **six Grands Prix remaining**, equalling Schumacher in 2002 [24].
  - It was the first title decided on a Saturday since Piquet at Kyalami in 1983 [24].
  - The next day he won the Qatar GP [7].

### 2.4 2024: São Paulo, Las Vegas and the winless stretch

- **São Paulo GP (3 Nov 2024):**
  - He qualified 12th and took a 5-place penalty for a new internal combustion engine, so he **started 17th** [17][28].
  - In a wet race with a red flag on lap 32, he passed Ocon for the lead at the lap-43 restart [28].
  - He won by 19.477 s from Ocon, with Gasly 3rd [17][28]. That is a gain of 16 places [31].
  - It was his first win since the Spanish GP in June [28].
- **Winless stretch:** **10 Grands Prix**, from Austria (30 Jun) to Mexico City (27 Oct) [10][31].
- **Las Vegas clinch (Sat 23 Nov 2024, local time):**
  - He needed only to stay ahead of Norris. He finished **5th**, with Norris 6th; Russell won [29].
  - It was round 22 of 24, so **2 GPs remained** (Qatar and Abu Dhabi) [10].
  - He became the sixth driver with four or more titles, after Fangio, Prost, Schumacher, Vettel and Hamilton [30].
- **Constructors:** Red Bull finished 3rd [12].

**RB16B (2021)**
- An updated 2020 RB16. Because of COVID, the new technical rules were postponed from 2021 to 2022, and 2020-spec cars were carried over [58].
- 2021 was Honda's final season as a works supplier. Honda announced its exit in October 2020 and brought forward a new power unit, the RA621H, for that last year [45][58].
- In 2021 Red Bull committed a "minor" overspend of the cost cap: £1,864,000, or 1.6%. The penalty, announced 28 Oct 2022, was a US$7m fine and a 10% cut in aerodynamic testing allowance. The FIA stated there was "no accusation or evidence" of bad faith [35].

**RB18 (2022)**
- The first car of the ground-effect era.
  - The 2022 rules brought back shaped underfloor (venturi) tunnels. Full ground-effect cars had been outlawed after 1982 [33].
  - The aim was closer racing. Downforce lost when following at 20 m dropped from about 35% to 4%, and at 10 m from 47% to 18% [33].
  - The rules also introduced 18-inch wheels and raised the minimum weight from 752 kg to 790 kg [33].
- It won Red Bull's first constructors' title since 2013 [6][59].
- Engine name: Honda officially left F1 after 2021, so the 2022 units were entered as "RBPT". HRC still built them [43][62].

**RB19 (2023)**
- It won 21 of 22 races (95.45%). This beat the McLaren MP4/4's 15 of 16 (93.75%) from 1988 [26][60].
- The Honda name returned to the power unit entry as "Honda RBPT" [43][60].

**RB20 (2024)**
- It won 7 of the first 10 races, then went 10 races without a win [10][31].
- It was the last Red Bull designed under Adrian Newey [61]. His departure was announced on 1 May 2024, and he left his CTO role in Q1 2025 [34].

Engine development was frozen from 2022 to the end of 2025 [47].
